See the geometry, not just the eclipse path

Many eclipse resources present a 2D path on a map or a table of contact times. This simulator is designed to make the spatial geometry visible: explore the shadow cones, their intersection with an Earth modeled as a WGS84 ellipsoid, and the resulting path over real Cesium terrain.

Features

Astronomical computation, geospatial visualization and interactive exploration in one desktop application.

🌘 3D eclipse geometry

Visualize umbra, penumbra and antumbra cones, together with the path of totality or annularity as a 3D band.

🌍 WGS84 Earth and Cesium terrain

Place the computed shadow geometry on an Earth modeled as a WGS84 ellipsoid, with terrain and imagery streamed through Cesium for Unreal.

🗓️ Long-range eclipse catalogues

Search and filter 71,264 solar and 72,272 lunar eclipses spanning 13,000 BC to 17,000 AD.

⏱️ Contact times and telemetry

Explore C1–C4 contact times, magnitude, coverage, altitude, azimuth and other astronomical information.

📍 City visibility

Inspect cities from which an eclipse is visible; for the 12 August 2026 solar eclipse, the documented catalogue lists 2,341 cities in 73 countries.

🌌 Night sky and solar system

Explore 5,079 naked-eye stars, constellations, planets and selected natural satellites.

🌦️ Weather and atmosphere controls

Experiment with configurable clouds, fog, rain and snow effects. These are visualization controls, not operational weather forecasts.

🌙 Lunar limb model

A compact hash-grid neural elevation model trained on NASA LRO/LOLA data supports lunar-limb corrections. Its documented RMSE against the gridded DEM is 12.57 m.

Scientific methods and validation

The simulator combines ephemeris data with custom geometry and numerical procedures. The figures below describe documented tests; they should not be interpreted as a universal accuracy guarantee across the full 30,000-year interval.

JPL DE441 and Skyfield

Celestial positions are obtained from JPL DE441 ephemerides through the Skyfield Python library.

WGS84 cone–ellipsoid intersection

The shadow geometry is intersected with an ellipsoidal Earth model rather than assuming a perfect sphere.

Time scales and ΔT

The backend handles time-scale conversion and calendar reconciliation for the historical catalogue.

Contact-time comparisons

Documented C1–C4 comparisons against Xavier Jubier for selected total solar eclipses (1999, 2026 and 2027) show maximum differences of up to 1.7 seconds across the tested cases.

Validation is limited to the documented events and locations. The application does not claim one-second independent validation for every eclipse over the entire catalogue range. See the repository’s validation files for comparison data and methodology.

For the implementation details, reproducibility materials, limitations and installation instructions, see the public source repository.

How it works

A two-tier architecture keeps astronomical computation separate from real-time rendering.

┌────────────────────────────────┐       UDP       ┌────────────────────────────────┐
│ Unreal Engine 5                │ ◀─────────────▶ │ Python backend                 │
│ C++ · UI · rendering · Cesium  │                 │ Skyfield · JPL DE441 · geometry│
│ camera · effects · telemetry   │                 │ contact times · city visibility│
└────────────────────────────────┘                 └────────────────────────────────┘

The Python backend computes astronomical data and sends compact binary UDP packets to Unreal Engine. Unreal Engine renders the scene and Cesium provides geospatial terrain and imagery.
